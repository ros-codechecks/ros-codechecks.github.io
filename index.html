Request a CODECHECK See how it works

A CODECHECK is an independent check that your code and data reproduce what you reported. It doesn't judge the science or review your code. It confirms that the computation works.

You provide a runnable version of your code and access to your data. An experienced codechecker does the rest.

How a CODECHECK works

  1. You prepare your code and data

    Make sure someone else can run your analysis without your help. Your repository should contain the code, the data or instructions for getting access to it, the software and package versions you used, the steps to run it, and which outputs correspond to which results in your paper.

  2. A codechecker reruns your workflow

    Following the community CODECHECK procedure, the codechecker runs your code and compares the outputs with the results you reported. If something breaks, they get in touch.

  3. You get a certificate

    A successful check produces a public, citable certificate on Zenodo, an entry in the CODECHECK Register, and a badge for your repository.

Your code doesn't have to be perfect. If the codechecker gets stuck, they'll contact you to sort it out. Every obstacle we record helps the study.

The study

Many researchers share code, yet others still struggle to rerun it. We want to know why. In this study we carry out CODECHECKs on research from participating institutes and record every obstacle a codechecker meets on the way: missing files, undocumented dependencies, environments that won't build, outputs that differ from the paper.

From those records we work out which qualities of research software matter most for reproducibility. The results will feed into concrete recommendations for software training and open science policy, at our own institutes and beyond.

Every CODECHECK you request gives you a certificate and adds a data point to this research. We aim to complete around 70 checks.

For codecheckers

Codecheckers in the study follow a shared standard operating procedure. Each check is limited to four hours of hands-on work; time a computer spends running unattended does not count. Along the way, codecheckers log the obstacles they run into in a structured form, so checks across institutes can be compared.

Interested in becoming a codechecker, or already checking at your institute and want to take part? Email ros-codecheck@amsterdamumc.nl.
